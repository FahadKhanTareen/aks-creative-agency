VR and AR activations

Headset walkthroughs, phone-based augmented reality and floating-image displays for sales suites, exhibitions and launches.

AKS CREATIVE STUDIO← Services and regionsView projects ↗

Not every project needs a building-sized show. Some audiences are better reached one person at a time: a buyer walking through an apartment that is not built yet, a visitor pointing a phone at a stand, a crowd watching an image float in mid-air. These formats use the same design and 3D skills as projection mapping at a smaller scale.

Formats

  • Virtual reality: headset walkthroughs for property sales, planning reviews and exhibitions.
  • Augmented reality: content anchored to a real place or object and seen through a phone or tablet.
  • HoloFans and mesh: displays that make an image appear to float free of any surface, for stands, lobbies and stages.
  • Combined: a projection mapped facade outside and VR or AR inside the same event.

Where they work

Property launches and sales centres, where buyers need to understand space and views before construction. Trade stands, where a short, personal experience draws people in. Museums and visitor centres. Brand events that want something guests can try, not just watch. The immersive event technology guide compares these formats with projection and LED. For planning detail, see planning a VR experience, planning an AR activation and when to use holographic fans and mesh screens.

What makes an activation work

  • Keep sessions short. Three to five minutes per person keeps a queue moving and avoids discomfort in a headset.
  • Let the user move, not the camera. Forced camera movement in VR causes motion sickness; walking, teleporting or a fixed viewpoint does not.
  • Plan the operation: staff to fit headsets and explain controls, hygiene covers, spare charged devices and a reset routine between guests.
  • Test AR in the real space. Tracking depends on light, surfaces and phone models, so test at the venue at the same time of day as the event.
  • Measure it. A sign-up or a lead form at the end turns an activation into something a sales team can follow up.

What to send

The audience and how many people will use it per hour, the space it runs in, the devices you have or want to hire, and the drawings or 3D models for any building or product that has to appear. Throughput matters: a headset experience serves far fewer people per hour than a screen, so the event plan decides the format as much as the idea.
