Content for LED facades and media walls

Animation made for the real pixel layout of a screen or facade, so it reads from the street and holds up on a loop.

Many buildings now carry a permanent LED facade or a large media wall. The hardware is expensive; the content is often an afterthought, stretched from a TV advert or a social video. Content made for the actual canvas, its pixel map, its shape and where people will see it from, looks far better on the same screen.

The studio was founded in 2019 by projection mapping artist Fahad Khan Tareen. His work took 2nd place at the World Championship of Projection Mapping on the TV Tower at the Festival of Lights in Berlin in 2019, created as lead artist with Artec, and the studio has since mapped Parliament House in Islamabad, Mohatta Palace in Karachi, Minar-e-Pakistan in Lahore and the Pakistan Monument. The same rules that make a projection fit a building make LED content fit a facade.

What the work covers

  • Reading the pixel map: resolution, pixel pitch, irregular shapes and gaps between panels.
  • Design for viewing distance, so type and detail survive from across the road.
  • Loops and schedules: content that can run for hours without feeling repetitive, with versions for day and night.
  • Seasonal and campaign packs: national days, holidays and brand campaigns.
  • Delivery in the format the playback system needs, tested before handover.

Projection or LED?

If you are still choosing the hardware, projection mapping or LED facade: how to choose compares brightness, cost over time, how much each changes the look of the building and what approvals each needs. Pixel pitch and viewing distance for LED facades explains how resolution and distance interact.

What makes LED content work

  • Brightness: an LED facade at full output is far too bright at night. Content and playback levels should be set for dusk and darkness separately.
  • Black is a design tool. On a facade, black pixels are switched off and the building shows through, so dark areas can frame the architecture rather than waste the screen.
  • Scale and speed: thin lines, small type and fast movement break up at a distance and on coarse pixel pitch. Fewer, larger forms moving more slowly read better from the street.
  • Shape: an irregular or wrapped facade needs content composed for its corners and gaps, not a rectangle cropped to fit.
  • Location: screens beside busy roads may have rules on flashing or fast-changing content. Check them before the concept, not after.

Working with an existing screen

For an installed facade, send the pixel map or the technical sheet, photos from where people will stand, and the playback system's format requirements. Content can then be produced remotely, previewed on a mock-up of the facade, and delivered ready to load.

Can you make content for a screen that is already installed?

Yes. With the pixel map and playback specification, content is produced remotely and delivered ready to load.

Do you make looping content for permanent screens?

Yes. Long-running loops are planned with variation and day and night versions so they do not tire the people who pass every day.
