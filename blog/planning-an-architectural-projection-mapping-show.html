How to plan an architectural projection mapping show

A practical production roadmap from the first site survey to show night.

BY FAHAD KHAN TAREENOCTOBER 4, 20267 MIN READ
AKS FIELD NOTES← All articlesView projects ↗

A practical production roadmap from the first site survey to show night. This field note explains the decisions that keep an ambitious visual idea clear, practical and reliable at event scale.

Begin with the purpose

A projection mapping project needs a clear reason to exist. A launch, civic celebration and permanent installation ask for different pacing, content and operating plans. Define the audience, viewing distance, show duration, repeat schedule and desired response before discussing equipment. These choices become the brief against which every later decision can be tested.

Survey the site early

Visit the site in the same lighting conditions expected during the event. Record accurate dimensions, surface materials, ambient light, projection positions, power access and audience sightlines. Photograph obstructions and note permissions or working-hour restrictions. A useful survey reduces creative surprises and prevents expensive technical changes late in production.

Connect creative and technical planning

The content canvas depends on the projector layout, resolution and overlaps. Build the technical plan and storyboard together. When the team knows where brightness drops or architecture becomes complex, it can place detail where it will remain legible and reserve large moments for the strongest areas of the façade.

Create milestones that reveal risk

Use staged approvals: architectural template, visual direction, animatic, representative finished scenes and final master. Test a short sequence through the real playback pipeline before producing the complete film. This exposes codec, colour, resolution and synchronization problems while they are still easy to correct.

Protect calibration and rehearsal time

Mapping the projectors is detailed work. Schedule enough darkness for alignment, blending, colour matching and full-show playback. Include time to review the experience from the audience position. A technically correct image can still need pacing or contrast changes when seen at architectural scale.

Plan the live operation

Document startup, playback, shutdown and recovery procedures. Confirm who calls the show and who responds to power, signal or weather issues. Test backups under realistic conditions. The audience should experience one confident performance, even when the system behind it is complex.
