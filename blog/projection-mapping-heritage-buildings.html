Projection mapping on heritage buildings: design with respect

How to create contemporary spectacle while respecting a historic site and its story.

BY FAHAD KHAN TAREENOCTOBER 4, 20267 MIN READ
AKS FIELD NOTES← All articlesView projects ↗

How to create contemporary spectacle while respecting a historic site and its story. This field note explains the decisions that keep an ambitious visual idea clear, practical and reliable at event scale.

Understand what the place represents

A heritage façade carries memory before any image reaches it. Research the site's history, symbolism and relationship with its community. Speak with curators, historians and local stakeholders where possible. The strongest concept grows from the building's identity rather than treating it as a generic screen.

Protect the physical structure

Installation planning must respect conservation requirements. Avoid unapproved fixings, loads or cable routes. Keep equipment, heat and public movement within agreed zones. Early coordination with site managers prevents a visually ambitious idea from creating physical risk.

Separate evidence from interpretation

Historical storytelling often combines archival material with artistic animation. Make that distinction clear. Use verified names, dates and images, and avoid presenting an imaginative scene as documented fact. Creative interpretation can be powerful when the audience understands its role.

Let architecture lead transitions

Ornament, masonry and symmetry provide natural transition points. A carved border can become a timeline; a dome can hold a celestial sequence; repeated arches can carry visual rhythm. These gestures celebrate the craft already present in the structure.

Balance spectacle and dignity

Not every heritage story needs relentless impact. Space for stillness, voice, music and carefully paced light can create emotional weight. Consider how colour and sound relate to the site's cultural meaning and to the tone of the event.

Leave the site as you found it

Plan installation and removal with the same care as the show. Document equipment positions, manage public safety, and inspect the site after strike. A successful heritage project creates a vivid temporary memory without leaving a physical trace.
